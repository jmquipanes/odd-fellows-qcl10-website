Encampments

The Encampment branch of the Independent Order of Odd Fellows is an advanced or higher degree within Odd Fellowship, designed to deepen a member’s understanding of its teachings beyond the lodge level. It was established in the early 19th century, with the first official Encampment in the United States organized in 1827, again through the leadership of Thomas Wildey. Its creation marked the development of a more structured system of higher instruction within the Order.

The term “Encampment” is drawn from a military or traveling camp, symbolizing a journey and temporary resting place. In Odd Fellowship, it represents the idea that members are pilgrims or travelers, continuing their moral and spiritual journey. While the lodge teaches the foundational principles of Friendship, Love, and Truth, the Encampment expands these teachings by emphasizing virtues such as Faith, Hope, and Charity, along with themes of perseverance, humility, and trust in a higher purpose.

Members of the Encampment are known as Patriarchs, and the branch itself operates with its own officers, rituals, and degrees, separate from the subordinate lodge. The degrees of the Encampment are more reflective and symbolic, focusing on life’s journey, the importance of faith during trials, and the hope of ultimate reward for a virtuous life. These lessons are often presented through allegory, encouraging members to look beyond material concerns and toward spiritual growth.

In essence, the Encampment represents a continuation of the Odd Fellow’s journey — moving from the practical teachings of the lodge to a more contemplative and philosophical path. It reinforces the idea that a true Odd Fellow is not only active in charity and brotherhood but is also a traveler seeking deeper meaning, guided by faith, sustained by hope, and committed to charity.
