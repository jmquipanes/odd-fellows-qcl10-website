Patriarchs

In the Independent Order of Odd Fellows, Patriarchs are members of the Encampment branch, which represents the higher or advanced level of Odd Fellowship. After a brother has progressed through the subordinate lodge, he may be admitted into the Encampment, where he is then referred to as a Patriarch. This title signifies a deeper commitment to the teachings of the Order and a continued journey toward moral and spiritual growth.

The term “Patriarch” is symbolic, drawn from ancient traditions where patriarchs were seen as wise leaders, guides, and heads of families or tribes. In Odd Fellowship, it reflects a member who is no longer just learning the basic principles of Friendship, Love, and Truth, but is now embracing higher virtues such as Faith, Hope, and Charity. These virtues are central to the Encampment degrees and emphasize trust in a higher purpose, endurance through life’s trials, and service to others.

As members of the Encampment, Patriarchs participate in more reflective and allegorical teachings. The lessons often focus on life as a journey — where one is a traveler passing through challenges, guided by faith and sustained by hope. The Encampment setting itself, symbolizing a camp or temporary resting place, reinforces this idea that life is not permanent, but a passage toward something greater.

In essence, a Patriarch in Odd Fellowship represents maturity in the Order — a member who continues to grow, not only in knowledge and service, but also in wisdom, humility, and spiritual understanding.
