Rebekahs

The Rebekahs, the female branch of the Independent Order of Odd Fellows, originated in the 19th century as a way to formally include women in the principles and charitable work of Odd Fellowship. Initially, women participated informally, but through the efforts of leaders like Thomas Wildey, the Rebekah Degree was officially established in 1851. It later evolved into a more structured andindependent body, gaining full lodge status in 1891.

The name “Rebekah” comes from the biblical figure Rebekah, whose kindness and generosity symbolize the values upheld by the Order. The Rebekahs emphasize virtues such as charity, faith, hope, and service to others, while sharing the core principles of Friendship, Love, and Truth with the Odd Fellows.

Over time, the Rebekahs grew into a global organization, actively involved in charitable works like caring for orphans, the elderly, and communities in need. Today, they stand as an essential part of Odd Fellowship, representing compassion, inclusivity, and the vital role of women in the fraternity’s mission.
